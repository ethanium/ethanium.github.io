A spreadsheet is often the right place to start. It is quick to change, familiar to the team, and useful for getting a new process moving.

The trouble begins when the spreadsheet becomes the process itself: people keep separate copies, status is tracked in email, and every handoff depends on someone remembering what to do next.

## Signs the process needs more than a spreadsheet

Look for repeated friction rather than a particular number of rows or users:

- Several people update the same records, but no one is sure which copy is current.
- Staff re-enter information between spreadsheets, email, and other systems.
- Approvals and exceptions are handled differently each time.
- It takes too long to find the latest status or explain how a decision was made.
- A missed reminder or handoff can delay the work.

One signal alone does not mean the answer is custom software. First, map how the work actually moves and identify where the errors, delays, or duplicated effort happen.

## Start with one workflow

A useful first release focuses on one process and its essential information. That might mean a shared record, clear ownership, a small number of approval steps, and a reliable view of what is waiting or complete.

Keep the first version focused. Bring over only the data the process needs, make exceptions visible, and avoid automating steps the team has not agreed on yet. Integrations, reports, and additional modules can follow when they solve a demonstrated need.

The goal is not to replace every spreadsheet. It is to give one important workflow a dependable home, so people can find the information they need and move work forward without stitching together copies and inboxes.
